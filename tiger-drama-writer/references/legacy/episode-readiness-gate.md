# Episode readiness and downstream gate (V3.4)

Use this evidence-driven checklist on every revised batch, after automated length screening:
1. Freeze incoming exact state (positions, injuries, control of objects, power/resources, outstanding promises). Record numbers in ledger and confirm their units and the population/consumption equation.
2. Map each episode's setup, protagonist choice, antagonist counteraction, visible payoff, price and next-episode input. Do not award credit to clue-only or repeated endings.
3. Verify adversarial setup: if protagonist gets to touch a crucial object, show **why the enemy permits it**, what trap they planned, and how protagonist perceptibly alters the encounter. No automatic villain stupidity.
4. Identify every modified ending's downstream impact. Rewrite affected next episode scene, or mark that seam as unresolved and block FULL_SCRIPT/INTEGRATED acceptance. Never silently claim closure.
5. Inspect conversational volume and action density as editorial warnings only; use actual timed read-through to claim timed runtime. A one-line-per-scene draft is SCENE_DRAFT regardless of numeric thresholds.
6. Keep user-locked facts distinct from candidate people, mechanisms and resources; generated material must never silently become LOCKED_USER.
7. Report fixed defects, remaining blockers, change impact and honest coverage; stop inflating successive versions for merely making a report.


### V3.6 action-pressure repair regression

Before calling a short episode repaired, compare the original and replacement at the **scene causality** level. Demand a changed tactical choice, an opponent/environment counteraction, an irreversible cost and a concrete new state; do not treat adding a fourth scene as success by itself. In multi-episode water/medicine/property arcs, record explicitly whether a machine was restored, whether the output passed safety testing, and whether stock increased or only existing stock was protected. Mark any ambiguous machine, resource or user-canon evolution as unresolved. A cliffhanger rewritten in episode N must be reconciled against the opening action of N+1. Record evidence as scene IDs. For short drama 2–3 minute target, reject runtime assertions based only on count; TIMED requires actual read-through.
