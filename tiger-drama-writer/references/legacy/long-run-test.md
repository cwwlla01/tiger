# Long-run series test rubric

Maintain a table per episode with: scene-action anchors, causal advancement, payoff kind, protagonist cost, antagonist rational reaction, unresolved question, new irreversible state, production-risk tag.

Reject and rewrite when:
1. Three successive episodes are primarily people viewing evidence or explaining paperwork.
2. A protagonist's victory is unearned (anonymous clue without provenance, coincidence, sudden unexplained power).
3. A major reveal does not alter options/relationships/information asymmetry.
4. A formerly locked outcome is simply reset for another identical fight.
5. Every cliffhanger is the same class (mysterious message, door opening, interrupted answer).
6. AI generatability requires small exact words rendered by video generator, a dense crowd continuity sequence, or a multi-object choreography in a single shot.
7. An estimated 90–120-second episode contains only a short synopsis, not enough playable dialogue and physical beats.

Deliver test status separately:
- Structural season test: full requested episode grid, with failure map.
- Script duration test: only fully scripted episodes can be time-budgeted.
- Shot feasibility: paper evaluation only until generated videos are inspected.
- Viewer retention: untested unless actual audience data is supplied.

No invented empirical percentages or false claims of production validation.
