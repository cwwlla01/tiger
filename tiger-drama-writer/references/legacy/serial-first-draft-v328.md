# V3.28 continuous first-draft realization protocol

This is an output-production protocol, NOT another claim of quality certification.

## Five consecutive episodes: apply before and during actual drafting

1. Freeze the immediately preceding episode's final states (injury, inventory, knowledge, ally, opponent, ability cost). State exact quantities and units; do not count contaminated/quarantined inventory as usable.
2. For each episode write three distinguishable dramatic exchanges, with scene roles: external challenge, active counterstroke, consequential settlement or escalation. These are design functions rather than a rigid number of locations.
3. Write one scene **at a time**. For each, convert the beat into playable action with an actual constraint, a first approach that fails or partially works, a tactical or relational counterchoice, material/emotional cost, and an altered after-state. Include an authentic reaction from another character; do not pad with synonyms or administrative forms.
4. Before starting the next episode, run the screenplay auditor on the single *unedited* finished episode. If it is flagged for short text or dialogue poverty, **stop the batch's claim of first-pass success**. Do not silently add words and continue under the first-pass label. Preserve snapshot and label any expansion as a revised draft. The generator should proactively realize scenes at adequate depth before declaring the initial pass finished.
5. Maintain an episode-to-episode ledger after each episode: usable water balance, separate unverified water, consumed tools, treatment/injury, powers used and their backlash, evidence status, and antagonists' observed knowledge. Every episode must have at least one verified positive result or costly reversal; information alone is not a universal genre payoff.
6. In power-up fiction, *do not force a new unlock every episode*. Distinguish: paid tactical demonstration of an established ability, upgrade/progression, and explicitly deferred upgrade promise. If the character does not use the ability, record deliberate non-use and its cost, not fictitious evidence of tactical deployment.
7. At the end of five episodes, preserve raw texts and per-episode raw flags. Run adversarial and batch audits, then independently review the causal state ledger and genre contract. A structural pass is reviewable text only; actual duration is UNTIMED absent a measured performance.

## Failure policy

- If 4/5 episodes flag `SHORT_TEXT_REVIEW`, report the batch as a **first-pass failure** even if the story causality is plausible. Do not retroactively pass the first draft after making revisions.
- If the same shortening pattern repeats, modify pre-draft execution allocation to prioritize fully realized scenes before attempting the whole batch; reduce parallel episode generation and avoid writing full-season meta commentary ahead of actual scene text.
- Do not claim genre performance based on only one new story or one pilot. Keep unedited first-pass as a fixed regression artifact.
