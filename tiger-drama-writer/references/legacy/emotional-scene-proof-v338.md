## Writing protocol
Before drafting each scene, outline privately: (a) concrete object at risk *right now*, (b) individual priorities that collide, (c) first attempted act with a visible stage consequence, (d) countermeasure distinct from a verbal objection, (e) changed tactic that costs money, trust, time, safety, evidence or status, (f) emotional aftereffect and its material trace carried into next scene. Stage these six beats **in dramatized action and conversation**, not narration or an end-of-scene recap.

After each scene, inspect the *actual written scene*: Can an actor play at least two changes of tactics? Did the outcome irreversibly change a document, object, decision, relationship, or risk? Do characters have distinct speech and boundaries? If not, write the missing interaction immediately **before** starting another scene; keep the saved raw first attempt separate from any revision. Avoid mechanically padding dialogue, enforced universal minimum scene length, and invented conflicts that break canon.

When creating an entire batch, perform scene-depth checks after each episode, not only after the last. An episode that is still short must be labeled a failed first pass; do not force a pass via text filler. Maintain a raw untouched version and transparent revision counts. Include adjacent-episode consequences in a concise ledger. A repaired existing manuscript is *not* a fresh-generation regression test.
