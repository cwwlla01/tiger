# V3.36: emotional/revenge episode completion and non-repetitive payoff

Use while drafting, not solely as a post-hoc checklist. Emotional scenes are scenes of **choices under a relationship cost**, not speeches about feelings.

## Scene realization procedure

1. Set an immediate physical or procedural constraint (a document that must be signed, a moving van, an expiring appointment, a witness who must leave). State who can decide and what happens if no one acts.
2. Each lead must have a separate *behavioral* priority: e.g. protagonist preserves verifiable evidence; sibling protects family honor but risks destroying it; elder worker protects unpaid wages and feels shame; lawyer protects legal viability at the expense of speed. Do not flatten them into cooperative evidence analysts.
3. Put one tangible choice on-page and show it being made while someone visibly disagrees. Let it change access, money, safety, allegiance or time.
4. After the choice give an emotional aftershock that changes the NEXT encounter. A hug or apology with no subsequent behavior is not a settled payoff.
5. Never mechanically cycle misunderstanding -> clarification -> new misunderstanding. Between two confrontations with the same pair, require an intervening change in verifiable facts, material leverage, or costly relationship commitment.
6. For revenge, alternate **evidence, leverage, relationship, and personal cost**. Do not announce guilt before an authenticated document or credible testimony supplies it. Do not make legal aid/filing magically suspend a property dispute.

## Anti-compression during long batches

After each drafted scene calculate approximate depth (not a prose-length quota), identify *missing action, distinct opposition, or aftermath* and fully realize that dramatic unit before starting the next. After each episode check the episode does not collapse into 3 short dialogue exchanges. When an episode is flagged, expand its exact weak scenes before proceeding; save the unedited first attempt separately and label subsequent completion as revision. If context/output limits force shorter output, continue in further files or clearly label remaining unwritten. Never bulk-output an abbreviated five episodes and call it a finished season.

## Evidence-based acceptance

Report per episode first-pass status, per scene weak flags, and separately editorial judgments about voice recognizability and emotional payoff. “No structural flags” is not audience approval. A single novel five-episode trial is not proof of cross-genre reliability. Include counterexample when an apparently kind choice is also strategic; avoid flawless heroes. Keep time unverified until a read-through is timed.
