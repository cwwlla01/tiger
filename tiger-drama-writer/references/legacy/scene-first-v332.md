# V3.32 single-scene realization protocol

Before writing a scene: (1) give the lead a concrete, observable task, (2) name the counterparty and what they lose if the lead succeeds, (3) put a verifiable object or deadline on stage, (4) write a failed or contested first tactic, (5) force a revised choice with bounded risk and a price, (6) record a verified outcome distinct from speculation, and (7) specify what the next scene inherits.

Do NOT replace the scene with its six-step outline. Write spoken lines and playable physical action and keep track of actual prop ownership, authority, medical/technical limits and evidence provenance.

Negative: A nurse asks whether to release oxygen. Hero says no, asks for verification. Stranger arrives. (Only states a policy.)

Positive causal structure: A transport team arrives with a valid electronic stamp and a dispatch deadline; nurse fears liability and needs explicit signoff; hero verifies that only reserve cylinders, not active treatment, would be transferred; supervisory authority confirms suspension and a documented responsibility chain; the team departs without bottles; an authentic printed record and dormant terminal identity remain to investigate.

Diagnostic gates: Run scene depth and release gate on frozen draft. When the first scene is thin, repair that scene's action/opposition/choice/outcome; don't lengthen the closing scene to manipulate episode total. Show original and repaired counts separately. Structural thresholds are only editorial screeners.
