# V3.40 Scene action and serial compression remediation

Use this *before* prose drafting, not as a post-hoc way to increase character counts.

## Playability and action outside dialogue

Every scene needs observable behavior not merely speakers explaining past events. For each scene, precommit to (1) one manipulable prop or physical constraint; (2) an attempt using it; (3) an opponent's counter-action; (4) a visible changed state; and (5) a cost or relationship shift. Express these in sparse, shootable action paragraphs placed among dialogue, not in abstract narrator commentary. A genuinely static, speech-dominant scene is acceptable when the negotiation itself changes an obligation, relationship, evidence custody or access, but the exception must be supported by an on-page event.

When batch screening flags INSUFFICIENT_ACTION_TEXT_REVIEW, **inspect scene-by-scene**. Do not satisfy the numeric condition by inserting atmosphere or empty stage directions. Add only concrete, consequential actions based on existing established props and character knowledge. Preserve evidence chain, inventory, and injuries.

## Serial compression under an exceptionally long pilot

Compare later episode scene-by-scene dramatic realization, not only total text against an unusually long opening episode. `check_serial_decay.py` emits a heuristic shrink warning. It does not prove EP04 is bad or require every episode to match the pilot's word count. For every warning inspect: tangible pressure; changed tactic; adversary response; price paid; episode state delta; emotional or genre payoff; episode hook. If these are all genuinely present and the shorter episode remains playable, retain the warning as editorially reviewed rather than mechanically padding. Otherwise revise the failing scene, saving the prior version immutably.

## Release claims

The structural batch gate, the adversarial action-text warning, and the relative-length decay warning are distinct. Report them separately and never call the whole batch ready when any is unresolved. Preserve original-first-pass results. Human editorial review and measured performance duration remain separate, unverified unless actually conducted.
