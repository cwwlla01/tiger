# V3.24 Scene realization protocol: screenplay, not beat summaries

**Use this during drafting, not only after the script is finished.** A plan may say 'hero finds evidence and persuades the gatekeeper'; that is not a scene.

## Scene construction (for each scene, in order)
1. Specify **observable present-tense task**: what the focal character physically needs to do in this location, now (not merely 'find out the truth').
2. Give an opposing character or environmental mechanism a **specific, credible way to stop it**. State the opposing party's stakes privately; don't make the obstruction vanish with a speech.
3. Write the **first physical attempt** and the other party's countermeasure on the page. Dialogue should change tactics or reveal a choice; no consecutive exchange that merely restates a position.
4. Force a **revision of tactics or a costly choice**. Have the character use an established object, relationship, limited resource or knowledge, rather than a convenient new power.
5. Render the **material after-state**: who holds the key, what was damaged, what was agreed and witnessed, what remains unproved. If negotiation fails, the new refusal/cost is itself a changed state.
6. Write a **scene-out button** that forces a different next action, not a generic noise or anonymous phone notification. Connect the next scene's start to this changed state.

## Expansion procedure when a raw episode is thin
- Do not paste a new conversation onto the same inert decision. Return to the missing beat (attempt, counteraction, costly pivot, aftermath) and dramatize it.
- **Never** invent a source of power, new resources, or new character knowledge to make the scene appear more exciting.
- Maintain per-scene state-in and state-out in a compact private table. If identical across two adjacent scenes without a purposeful costly standoff, recompose the scene.
- A high dialogue count, 900 Han characters, and clean automation checks are **minimum review signals**; they do not establish a full script or performed duration.
- When a user requests many episodes, write one complete episode before the next, archive raw first attempts, and do not claim all episodes generated if output budget was insufficient.

## Anti-example and corrected miniature
BAD: '她向警卫说明情况。警卫不信。她继续解释。警卫终于放行。' This hides the persuasion mechanism and the cost.

BETTER (illustrative only, not a substitute for full scene): '门闩已落，警卫伸手要收走通行牌。她把沾油的阀门垫圈放在桌上，指明背面断裂处；警卫仍拒绝，除非有人为开门承担罚款。她在登记簿写下自己的姓名，签字时左手伤口崩开。警卫亲自对照断口与仓库清单，开一人宽的小门，扣下她的通行牌作为担保。' This gives opposition, evidence, costly choice and an observable changed state.

## Evidence protocol for generator improvements
- Freeze premise, episode count, scope, checker version and raw draft before audits.
- Archive a new raw draft separate from repaired output. Old different-premise samples are historical context, **not controlled A/B**.
- Run both static auditors plus release gate on full episodes; record structural status separately from editorial inspection and timed reading.
- Document explicit unresolved weaknesses. Do not claim a generator breakthrough from an added rule alone.
