# Faction transition and settlement pressure gate (V2.5)

Use for any story in which a protagonist conquers a faction or begins governing a settlement.

- Treat winning the fight, taking the keys, feeding people, earning buy-in, securing utilities, and surviving external threats as distinct events. Do not call a faction stable immediately after the old leader falls.
- Make the next 5–10 episodes contain at least three different causal engines: internal legitimacy, material shortage and external threat, with person-to-person relationships woven through each. Each engine must be caused by a previous state, not a random visitor.
- Every 2–3 episodes show something the protagonist can **actually keep** (access, safe water, recruits, a reliable rule) and its ongoing cost (rationing, exposure, divided loyalty, illness). Avoid repeated empty victory declarations.
- Show dissenters' incentive to stay, defect or rebel. Never assume guards switch sides because a hero delivers a speech. Count real people and inventory; mark disputed numbers and props as awaiting reconciliation.
- A settlement leadership arc needs a non-combat reward and a new threat seeded from world canon. If the plot includes a remote intelligent antagonist, show bounded and gradually escalating surveillance; do not reveal its full powers prematurely.
- Continue explicit user canon. All newly named gangs, guards, locations and material rules stay PROPOSED unless user accepts them.
- Deliver actual scene-and-dialogue text for any episode claimed scripted. Mark condensed samples as SCENE_DRAFT, and never imply 2–3 min runtime without timed read-through.
